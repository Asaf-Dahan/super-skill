# Layer 1: Domain structure and sub-domain relationships
# How sub-domains connect and affect each other

## Sub-domains

### Sub-domain 1: Seasonal Calendar
What to plant, when to plant it, and when to harvest.
Driven by climate, frost dates, and daylight hours.
Decisions here affect all other sub-domains.

### Sub-domain 2: Soil Management
Soil composition, pH, compost, and amendments.
Affects which plants thrive and how much water is needed.

### Sub-domain 3: Water Systems
Irrigation method, frequency, and volume per bed.
Affects yield, soil health, and plant disease risk.

### Sub-domain 4: Pest and Disease Control
Identification, prevention, and organic treatment.
Affects which plants can grow near each other.

### Sub-domain 5: Crop Rotation
Which plants follow which in each bed each season.
Prevents soil depletion and breaks pest cycles.

## Cross-domain Relationships

A change in Seasonal Calendar affects:
  Soil Management (different crops need different amendments)
  Water Systems (summer crops need more water than winter crops)
  Crop Rotation (next season plan must account for current plantings)

A change in Soil Management affects:
  Water Systems (amended soil retains water differently)
  Seasonal Calendar (some crops only viable after soil preparation)

A change in Pest Control affects:
  Seasonal Calendar (some crops delayed or avoided due to pest pressure)
  Crop Rotation (pest-resistant varieties may be prioritized)

## What This Domain Does Not Cover

Fruit trees, ornamental plants, lawn, indoor plants,
and landscaping. Those are separate domains with their
own soil requirements, timelines, and expertise.
