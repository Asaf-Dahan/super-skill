# Layer 1: Domain structure and sub-domain relationships
# How sub-domains connect and affect each other

## Sub-domains

### Sub-domain 1: Asset Allocation
The target mix across asset classes.
The most consequential layer - changes here affect everything else.

### Sub-domain 2: Individual Holdings
Specific positions within each asset class.
Evaluated against the allocation targets in sub-domain 1.

### Sub-domain 3: Risk and Exposure
Concentration risk, currency exposure, sector overlap.
Monitored continuously - alerts when thresholds are breached.

### Sub-domain 4: Liquidity Management
Cash position, time to liquidate each holding.
Ensures obligations can be met without forced selling.

### Sub-domain 5: Tax Considerations
Capital gains, loss harvesting, holding periods.
Affects timing of all buy and sell decisions.

## Cross-domain Relationships

A change in Asset Allocation affects:
  Individual Holdings (positions must be adjusted to match new targets)
  Liquidity Management (rebalancing requires available cash)
  Tax Considerations (rebalancing triggers capital gains events)

A change in Individual Holdings affects:
  Risk and Exposure (concentration and sector exposure shift)
  Tax Considerations (any sale has tax implications)

A change in Risk and Exposure affects:
  Asset Allocation (may require rebalancing to restore targets)
  Individual Holdings (high-risk positions may need reduction)

## What This Domain Does Not Cover

Real estate, business equity, pension funds managed by employers,
and insurance products. Those require separate Super Skills.
